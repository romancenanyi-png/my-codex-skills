# Google Maps B2B Prospecting

Google Maps is a discovery surface, not proof that a company is a suitable buyer.

## Route setup

Define one product, one buyer type, and one city/region per route. Rotate buyer types such as distributor, wholesaler, corporate-gift supplier, promotional-products company, travel/outdoor retailer, school supplier, beauty distributor, or specialty retailer. Switch route when results become duplicates or low-fit.

## Funnel

1. Discover map businesses and record map URL, category, website, location, rating/activity signals, and route.
2. Reject giants/chains when the brief requires reachable independents, plus repair/service, printers, irrelevant packaging, no-site, no-product, and consumer-only businesses.
3. Open the official website and verify product fit, buyer role, recent activity/catalog, and procurement or outsourcing evidence.
4. Find a public decision maker. Prefer sourcing/procurement/product/merchandising/partnerships, then owner/founder for smaller companies.
5. Validate the contact from the official site or another attributable public business source. Never guess an email; phone does not equal WhatsApp.
6. Dedupe against the current run and historical outreach by company, domain, first email, person, phone, and aliases.

## Scoring

Score 100 points: contact quality 30, buyer identity 25, product fit 20, current activity 15, personalization angle 10. Default A threshold is 85. An official generic inbox may be retained for research but should not outrank a verified decision maker.

## Output

Return a discovery table, verified shortlist, exclusion log, dedupe log, route yield, and A-grade pilot list. Each accepted row needs source URLs, observation date, facts, inferences, score explanation, and contact status (`valid`, `needs review`, or `not usable`).

Research does not authorize outreach. If the route is saturated, recommend a different city/buyer type or a content-signal channel such as TikTok.
