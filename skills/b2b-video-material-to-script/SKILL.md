# B2B Video Material to Script

Read the whole material set before choosing a story. Preserve the process logic that makes the evidence credible.

## Inventory and review

1. Create a manifest with filename, duration, dimensions, frame rate, orientation, audio, and obvious defects.
2. Sample each clip at regular intervals for coarse classification, then inspect promising sections more closely.
3. Classify footage: facility/signage, sample wall, workshop, process, sewing/detail, trimming/QC, packing/warehouse/shipping, visit/meeting, product beauty, or weak/repetitive.
4. Record exact candidate in/out timecodes, why the segment matters, and any privacy, permission, watermark, or quality concern.

## Evidence ladder

- **Visible:** an action or object clearly shown.
- **Explainable:** a cautious process statement supported by visible context.
- **Not proven:** order, approval, partnership, nationality, certification, test result, defect rate, lead time, or commercial outcome absent from evidence.

Do not delete critical intermediate actions in a way that fabricates a process. A visit is not a testimonial; inspection is not acceptance; packing is not proof of shipment.

## Script construction

Choose one buyer, one product, one risk question, and one CTA. Create at least two genuinely different concepts—such as chronological documentary, buyer-observation, or dialogue-led—rather than cosmetic variants. Use hard cuts or motivated transitions, preserve useful source audio, and avoid decorative effects that weaken proof.

## Output

Return asset manifest, rejected/limited clips, evidence matrix, selected concept, shot table with source timecodes and target duration, on-screen text, voiceover if authorized, audio plan, claim flags, platform crops, and review checklist.

## Validation

Check every source clip is accounted for, every used statement is visible or documented, captions avoid covering hands/products, and total shot durations equal the intended runtime.
